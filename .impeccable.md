## Design Context

### Users

Developers who supervise local coding agents, browser work, desktop actions, delegated tasks, and code-memory workflows from one long-running desktop workspace. Their primary job is to start work quickly, understand what the agent is doing, and move between active tasks without losing context.

### Brand Personality

Calm, exacting, companionable. Bridge Codex should feel like a trusted workbench: quiet when nothing needs attention, explicit when an action or service needs intervention, and dense only where technical detail earns the space.

### Aesthetic Direction

Follow the information architecture and restraint of OpenAI's Codex desktop app rather than copying ornamental details: a project/task rail, a focused central thread, a substantial bottom composer, and contextual utility surfaces for browser, desktop, routing, delegation, and memory. Support light and dark themes with warm neutral surfaces, rare green accents, precise typography, low-radius controls, and minimal decorative effects. Avoid the current operations-dashboard feel, neon-on-black palette, permanent three-column density, generic cards, and exposed controls that are not relevant to the current task.

### Design Principles

1. Keep the current thread and next action visually dominant.
2. Reveal technical controls progressively through contextual tool panels.
3. Preserve every working capability; navigation must never be decorative.
4. Show state through plain language, restrained color, and useful recovery actions.
5. Make keyboard, focus, reduced-motion, and compact-window behavior first-class.
